# 5 Dead: midnight arena
User requested a full game-presentation upgrade referencing UNO Mobile: a landscape social arena, large overlapping cards, expressive avatars, dramatic action feedback, animated dealing and tactile audio. This replaces the first release's flat game table. The simple setup and underlying rules remain.

## Visual world
A luminous teal card table floating in a midnight fantasy sky. Original AI environment and six rounded 3D character portraits, rich navy shadows, pearl-rimmed avatars, warm gold turn markers, coral life loss, glossy saturated cards. Barlow Condensed remains the game lettering; DM Sans remains the supporting face. No UNO marks or artwork.

## Layout
The active game fills the screen. Opponents occupy the far, left and right sides. The player's fanned hand runs along the bottom, ordered numeric 7 down to 0 then wilds. The running total and actual discard pile own the center. A compact roster includes every player at larger tables. Phones use native landscape/fullscreen where available, with a CSS-rotated live landscape table fallback. Menus, privacy screens and results remain operable in the same orientation.

## Motion thesis
Focal moment: an action card flies into the shared pile, then its consequence plays across the table: bombs create expanding shockwaves and life reactions; reverse sweeps the table; draws send cards to recipients; clearing a hand and winning emit celebration particles.
Continuity: public server receipts identify actions without leaking private hands; a bounded queue sequences cues. Newly dealt hands fan in and selected cards lift. Active player portraits identify turn order; the 21 total and life losses remain meaningful without motion.
Feedback: distinct synthesized offline sounds for card movement, dealing, turn, draw, reverse, skip, reset, bomb, life loss and victory. Sound has a persistent mute control and is unlocked by a user gesture.
Budget: CSS transforms/opacity for moving cards and bounded particles; no 3D runtime or new animation dependency. Reduced motion removes spatial sequences while retaining cue text, color and short fades. Local bot turns wait for the current effect to finish.

## Rules
Whole 77-card decks scale at one per six players, preserving the existing 2–24 player implementation. Display sorting never alters a deal. Game effects and rules use the same authoritative engine.

## Direct card handling
Cards lift after 5px of movement with pointer capture and track the user's grip, without selecting or shifting the underlying hand. A broad upward toss of 16–24px (12px for a quick flick) plays from anywhere, even before reaching the felt; the existing flight carries it to the pile. No aiming or speed requirement applies to ordinary tosses. Double-clicking (or double-tapping) a card, dropping it anywhere on the visible table, or deliberately flicking toward it submits the same validated move as Play. A shared in-flight guard prevents repeated clicks or post-drag clicks from submitting twice. The release grip counts as a drop even when the card is held near its corner. Misses, cancelled gestures and rejected moves return to the original hand slot. Pointer coordinates account for the rotated portrait-phone layout. Only almost-horizontal touch swipes on a long hand scroll it; diagonal lifts remain throws; small motions remain taps. Return flight starts at the release position, including pass-and-play seat changes. Reduced motion preserves direct manipulation and uses a short fade for rejection.

## Phone ergonomics
The S24 Ultra and similar Android phones keep 48px setup icons, 52–56px setup actions and fields, and 16px form text. In play, a 52px header holds the wordmark, hand number, roster and a 44px options disclosure. Rules, log, sound, fullscreen and leaving live in that disclosure; a labeled clockwise/counterclockwise indicator remains visible in its own header space. The turn cue sits above the 48px Play button in the right gutter rather than crossing the hand. Cards fit their measured tray: a moderate fan shrinks as the count grows, then switches to two complete rows with at least 44px card widths. Extremely large hands swipe horizontally in those two rows, with an explicit hint; selection never expands sibling spacing. Forty pixels above and fourteen below the cards reserve space for lifting, outlines, badges and rotation. Opponents and the shared piles compact when the hand needs two rows. The installation manifest allows either orientation; entering a game requests landscape/fullscreen where supported. Portrait setup remains one readable form column. Native landscape and the CSS-rotated fallback share logical container measurements, with safe-area insets remapped through rotation. Browser emulation covers phone viewports and shrinking browser space; physical Samsung browser and cutout behavior require on-device verification.

## Card face clarity
Use the printed set’s saturated blue/cyan draw, lime reset, magenta reshuffle/pass, orange reverse, and red/black bomb cues, with original vector marks. Every face owns an inline-size container: a separate top index, central mark, and short action label scale with that face rather than the hand’s size. Numeric cards have mirrored indices and no footer; action cards omit the lower index so labels cannot collide with it. Set-value marks use a smaller type scale than single numbers. Bomb uses charcoal, a red field, gold frame and yellow fuse blast; Reshuffle shows cards surrounded by circular arrows. Rules display the same component as gameplay. Reshuffle and Bomb remain one per base deck; presentation changes must never bias dealing.
